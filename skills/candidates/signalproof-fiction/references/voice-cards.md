# Character Voice Cards and the Hidden-Tags Test

Character voices are **diagnostic profiles**, not dialogue generators or fixed catchphrases. Speech varies with fear, intimacy, rank, fatigue, education, and character growth.

## Voice-card fields

For each recurring speaker, capture:
- Dominant communication purpose: command, bargain, evade, provoke, soothe, interrogate, persuade.
- Sentence shape and length: fragments, spare clauses, long formal periods, ellipses by implication.
- Lexicon and imagery: professional trade, era, upbringing, rank, culture.
- Humor: when it appears, what it targets, and how often.
- Relationship-specific register: how this speaker addresses a superior, peer, lover, enemy, stranger.
- What they avoid saying, and what breaks that pattern under stress.
- Two source-backed quotes from the *author-approved* manuscript (store only in the private project profile for unreleased work).
- No-go patterns and approved exceptions.

## Generic distinct-role examples

These are illustrative prototypes, never automatically canonical for a real cast.

| Function | Diagnostic profile | Short invented sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experienced ship captain | Terse, weathered, nautical, dry humor in danger; speeches rare | "That seam won't survive another turn." |
| Ancient sovereign | Economical, old-fashioned gravity, silence as a choice; avoids self-explanation | "Then we hold." |
| Queen/commander | Clear orders, practical questions; warmth and dry wit in private | "You knew the answer before you asked." |
| Broker/intelligence operative | Quick bargaining logic, irony, pointed questions about price and leverage | "Who's paying for the mistake?" |
| Veteran drill instructor | Short imperatives, blunt corrections, praise sparingly earned | "Again. This time, watch your footing." |
| Courteous antagonist | Precise, bureaucratic civility concealing a threat | "I trust you will make this uncomplicated." |

## Blind speaker attribution

1. Select a representative exchange with at least three recurrent characters.
2. Remove speaker tags and identifying action beats on a temporary copy.
3. Ask a reader/editor to attribute lines with reasons.
4. Flag lines that could swap speakers without sounding strange.
5. Revise only the swappable lines, looking at role, situation, subtext, and character development.
6. Check that variation does not become a cartoon rule: the captain can speak at length when the moment demands it; the king can joke; the villain can lose composure.

## Editorial protection

The AI may **flag mismatches without rewriting** when the author requests diagnostic-only review. Distinguish a recommended voice change from an accepted authorial line. Do not impose one "AI-authentic" voice across a manuscript simply because the prose has already been collaboratively revised.
