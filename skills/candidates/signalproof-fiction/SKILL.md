# Signalproof Fiction (Candidate v0.1.0)

**Maturity:** Candidate. Proposed for review; not an Active public skill.
**Parent contract:** signalproof. **Public/private boundary:** This file is a general method. Unreleased manuscripts, exact story bibles, and character-specific voice cards belong in separately authorized private project records.
**Governing principle:** **Every word must serve the story.** There is no requirement that all AI-proposed wording be rejected. Judge the result by literary function, author intent, and continuity.

## Authority and scope

1. The author has final authority over canon, character identity, narrative intent, voice, stylistic exceptions, and acceptance. AI suggestions and reviewer feedback are proposals, not commands.
2. Read the **latest author-supplied manuscript**, the chapter's active outline, controlling story guide, accepted continuity/cosmology, and any editorial handoff before drafting or editing. If revisions disagree, compare their dates and authority; do not silently invent a reconciliation.
3. Lock the chapter boundary before editing. Work **one chapter at a time** unless explicitly authorized otherwise. Do not label an entire novel "remastered" after editing only one chapter. Preserve the original, make a distinct working copy, and report exactly what was changed.
4. Preserve plot causality, scene function, relationships, established historical setting, limits of power, and prior accepted fixes. Material alterations to those elements require an author's separate approval.
5. **Chapter length follows dramatic necessity.** Allow a chapter to grow, contract, split, or remain unchanged according to story, pacing, and stakes. Never pad to reach a word count or cut to meet an arbitrary target without an author-set limit.
6. Treat canon-specific voice, myth, timeline, names, unresolved mysteries, and protected reveals as private project data. No public publication or cross-project reuse without explicit permission.

## Chapter-development workflow

**A. Establish truth.** Confirm source manuscript version, chapter title, previous chapter's ending, next chapter's opening, timeline, cast, locations, rules, and promised reveals. List conflicts without fabricating resolutions.

**B. Diagnose before changing.** Identify story-level problems first: dramatic objective, opposing force, reversals, agency, emotional continuity, repeated beats, underdeveloped consequences, scene order, and exposition load. Distinguish fatal continuity issues, meaningful improvements, and optional taste.

**C. Build a bounded editing plan.** Preserve the existing scene architecture where it works. For proposed structural changes, describe what each scene gains or loses and why. Consider both cuts and expansions.

**D. Revise with the authorial filter.**
- **Cuts/sharpening:** Usually promising when they increase precision, pace, or impact. Still test them against necessary setup and meaning.
- **Additions:** Require a specific dramatic purpose. Added action, sensory consequence, conflict, subtext, or character-specific dialogue can stay. Added explanation, policy recap, hedging, or self-justifying narration usually goes.
- Never rewrite an entire paragraph for a local correction unless the broader rewrite is independently authorized.
- Let action, dialogue, and consequences carry meaning before adding summary.
- Avoid generic rhetorical habits: repeated "not X, but Y," stacked "Not this. Not that.", interchangeable polished speeches, repetitive moral conclusions, reflexive hedges, and surplus "pretend" constructions.
- Apply the project's punctuation rule. If the author forbids em and en dashes, do not introduce either; verify mechanically.

**E. Differentiate dialogue.** Prepare a brief card for each speaking character: word economy, register, favored vocabulary, rhythms, humor, emotional evasions, status/power dynamics, and two approved in-text exemplars. Test a scene with speaker tags hidden. Flag swappable lines first; propose exact replacements only within the approved revision scope. See [voice cards](references/voice-cards.md).

**F. Check consequences and continuity.** A rule should appear when it changes a decision or imposes a cost. Keep a full explanation where a reader needs it; remove later reminders that merely prove the author remembers it. Maintain realistic physical constraints within the story's world. Maintain each character's ability to act independently and make consequential choices.

**G. Verify and deliver.** Diff the full revised chapter against the controlling original. Inspect every addition, deletion, and changed claim. Record word counts accurately but never treat a count as an editorial target. Review scene transitions and the neighboring chapter boundary. Provide the *complete* clean chapter, visual/redline comparison when supported, and change record with accepted/pending/author-decision items. Do not imply revisions are canon-locked without author acceptance.

## Editorial gates

Use [revision gates](references/revision-gates.md) for the exact before/after test and [acceptance and handoff](references/acceptance-and-handoff.md) for verification. When working on a protected story project, consult its private project profile for exact character voice cards and canon.

## Stop conditions

Stop or isolate a proposed edit if it changes an accepted event, character motive, power limit, crucial clue, character death, timeline, or author-approved revelation without permission; leaks an unpublished manuscript into a public repository; reverts earlier fixes; replaces authorial character voice with generic measured diction; or reports a chapter or book as edited when no actual complete comparison was made.

## Candidate acceptance

- Run against one short dialogue-heavy scene and one complex continuity scene.
- Confirm no invented facts, no silent canon changes, and meaningful speaker differentiation.
- Compare revised text to original, including every changed paragraph.
- Confirm chapters are scoped honestly and no artificial length rule is imposed.
- Obtain author approval before promoting skill to Active or merging into canonical public state.
